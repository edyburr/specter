    Stowed, the shell is a truncated icosahedron: 19 hexagonal faces are array tiles, the 20th is the aft cap, and the 12 pentagonal gaps stay open as listening ports. Nineteen is one centre tile plus rings of 6 and 12, which is exactly what fills the 5 m aperture from the hull derivation; a 20th tile would have nowhere to go. In hinged mode the 19 array tiles unfold as a net: hexagons only share alternate edges, so the net is a three-armed pinwheel with open notches between the arms. Same radiating area as the filled array, so the same on-axis intensity; the main lobe is roughly 30% narrower but holds about half the power instead of 83%, with the rest in sidelobes. With boom-tip mounts the horns rise on posts until their mouths are level with the array face. With tile stalks there are no booms in hinged mode at all: each horn rides a short stalk on the back of a rim tile and flips forward once the tile is flat. With port webs the 12 horns sit in the 12 pentagonal gaps of the stowed shell, slung on steel wires that telescope and swivel at their anchors, a pair from the front and back of each corner of the gap to a corner of the horn's bevelled pentagonal lip; as the petal opens each gap becomes an empty hexagonal cell and the horn stays at its centre. Dots on the tiles stand in for the real element lattice, which is far denser.
