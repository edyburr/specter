# The Orchid: Gundam design language

A brief for continuing the design in the cloud. It says what we're going for and what is already built. It also lists what's left.

## The idea

Minimalist, industrial Gundam. We are **not** making a humanoid mech. We borrow the look of the *tech in general*: the arm units, the joints and the hardware. Think Calibarn, a Bull G arm-unit manual and Fenrir. The colour scheme (Fenrir) is a Blender job for later.

What we like in those references:
- Geometric lines.
- Polygons, not just squares and hexagons.
- Cylinders, very few balls or spheres.
- Playing with the shape of things.
- Joints like a surgical robot (da Vinci / Maxon): cylinders, not balls.

Human-scale details (launch locks, handholds, stencils) come **after** the overall shape is nailed.

## The rule

> Engineering decides what's there. The style decides how it's drawn.
> Round only where something turns on it; everywhere else, polygons.

Gundam is not a licence to fake the physics. Every joint must still carry its load.

## Joint rule (load path)

A passive, unmotorised joint can only carry load through its axles. So:
- A strut needs a two-axle joint at each end, and the two axles must **cross on the strut's line**. This is the hexapod logic.
- Joints are centred drums (12-gon cylinders, `DRUM_N`) with square links, not offset side-by-side drums, because an offset puts torque on a passive joint.
- No balls. The spoke's roll is taken by its telescoping tube. The fibre loops round the axles, so there is no rotating seal.

## What is built

Commits `d380bfb`, `83a6695` and `7b1b7d7`.

- **Spoke ends:** a turret on the ear eye, plus a yoke carrying the end link on the cross axle. Together they read as one three-part drum. The spoke line passes through the crossing point.
- **Doubled horn corner:** the one corner per horn that takes two spokes on each side gets two eyes and a wider ear. Each spoke takes the eye on its own side.
- **Panel actuators:** each is a two-ended strut.
  - Bottom: a gimbal ring between the bracket legs. The ring turns on one axle and the cylinder turns in the ring on the other.
  - Top: a cube joint under the whiffle (socket arms on X, shaft yoke on Y).
  - Checked: all 120 struts stay on their lines to about 1e-15 in concave, convex and flat faces.
- Parts of one class share one mesh, so the Blender export can use the `part` tag. Names are stable (`<owner> / <label>`).

Lore the design started from is in `lore/` (the Firefly notes and the Specter dump). Treat it as spice, not as spec.

Code lives in `specter.html`. The joint helpers are `drumLink`, `acrossY`, `capY` and `capX`, placed just before `MOUNT`. `index.html` is generated by `sh tools/build_index.sh`. Don't edit it by hand.

## Soft-polygon pass

Helpers `loft`, `inset`, `soft`, `polyPts` and `hex30` sit just before the joints in `specter.html`. `soft(outline, z0, h, top, bot, holes)` is the workhorse: a plate with bevelled edges. Use it instead of a bare `slab` or `hexPrism` for anything plate-like.

- **Panels:** soft hexagons. Each corner is clipped one isogrid step back along both edges, so the cut runs rim node to rim node and a rib closes it. The face sheet is bevelled 12 mm in, and the panel line follows the clipped outline.
- **Horn bodies:** the prism draws in to a tail with a boss on its end (one `loft`, still one mesh per horn type).
- **Core:** the icosahedron is gone. The singularity's bound is a hexagonal crystal, blunt-pointed at both ends, on the craft's axis.
- **Inertial reference:** two bevelled steps, wide base on the aft cap and a neck the spine starts from.
- **Shadow shield:** bevelled both faces to a blunt rim, with a hub on each face round the mast.
- **Drums (settles the old hex-cylinder question):** what turns is a 12-gon: hinge barrels, hinge pins, actuator cylinders. What only slides stays hex: lift shafts. Actuator rods and spoke tubes also stay hex. They do roll, but at 10-12 mm across a 12-gon is just ink in the wireframe.
