    Design notes

    Stowed, the shell is a truncated icosahedron: 19 hexagonal faces are array tiles, the 20th is the aft cap, and the 12 pentagonal gaps hold the receive horns. Nineteen is one centre tile plus rings of 6 and 12, which is exactly what fills the 5 m aperture from the hull derivation; a 20th tile would have nowhere to go. Each tile is two layers, after the JWST mirror segments: a fold frame, ribbed open at the back, which is the structure, and a flat panel carrying the elements, held above it on six actuators. The 19 frames unfold as a net, hinged on shared edges, and latch flat: hexagons only share alternate edges, so the net is a three-armed pinwheel with open notches between the arms. Same radiating area as a filled array, so the same on-axis intensity; the main lobe is roughly 30% narrower but holds about half the power instead of 83%, with the rest in sidelobes. The frames only ever go flat; the face takes its shape from the actuators, which tip each panel in proportion to its distance from the axis, so the panel normals meet at one point on the axis for a concave face or spread from one behind it for convex. The panels do not also move up or down to the depth of a true dish: the elements make up that difference in phase. Each horn hangs at the centre of its gap on hollow 10 mm spokes that telescope and swivel at their ends, a pair from the front and back of each frame corner round the gap to the front and back of a corner of the horn's pentagonal face; as the petal opens each gap becomes an empty hexagonal cell and the horn stays at its centre. Each horn is a receive array of its own: the face carries the transducer elements and the body behind it the electronics. Its signal travels as light, in optical fibre inside the spokes: every spoke end turns on two axles that cross on the spoke's own line, and no ball: a turret turns on the ear's eye to follow the spoke round, and the spoke's end link tilts in a yoke on the turret, its drum between the yoke's two so the three read as one drum, while the round tube rolls in its telescoping sleeve. A joint that turns freely can only carry a load through its axles, so the spoke only ever pushes or pulls straight through the crossing. The fibre comes up the hollow eye into the turret and loops once round the yoke's axle, so nothing needs a rotating seal, and on into the frames and through their hollow edge hinges to the front tile and down the centre mast to the core. Every spoke reaches a frame, so each horn has several paths home, and no cable ever crosses to a moving panel but its own short service loop. Dots on the panels stand in for the real element lattice, which is far denser.
